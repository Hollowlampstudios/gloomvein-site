About the game

In one line

An idle mining RPG for Android. Dig down through eight layers, gear up, and beat the bosses that guard the gold.

In three paragraphs

The gold runs deeper than anyone has dug. So does whatever is guarding it.

Gloomvein is an idle mining RPG for Android. You dig a shaft down through eight layers of the world, from grassy surface to a lava floor, and every block you break is ore for the furnace and gear for your hero. Enemies come at you the whole way down, and each layer ends at a boss you cannot idle past: it takes the right gear, potions and timing.

Pick one of four classes, recruit a crew that keeps digging while you are away, and Rebirth into harder worlds once you reach the bottom. It is free, with no forced ads and nothing random for sale.

How it is made

Gloomvein is made by one developer, with AI tools, and we say so up front. AI does a lot of the drawing and the typing. Getting it to produce a game that looks like one game, plays fairly and holds together took a long chain of custom tools, checks and restarts. This is how it was done.

The art: the hardest part

Every character, block, item and background started as a painting made in Google Gemini: 363 source pictures, each prompted for one thing in a neutral pose on a flat background, so it could be cut out cleanly.

PixelLab turned each painting into pixel art and animated the characters. AI animation never comes out clean. It redraws the face in every frame, so mouths flicker. It adds smoke, sparks and slash trails nobody asked for, and sometimes they take a weapon or a jaw with them. It leaves a pale rim around the outline that shimmers as the character moves, and colours drift between takes (tin came back gold, a bat came back orange).

So the art runs through 19 tools written for this game. They cut every picture from its background, put every sprite on one shared pixel grid, line up the feet, keep only the clean frames, strip effects frame by frame, peel off the shimmering rim, and paste one steady face across each animation.

This is the fourth complete art set. Before it came hand-picked AI sheets, 3D models rendered in Blender, and a high-resolution set made on a local image model; each was replaced when it did not hold up on a phone. The game now holds 421 sprite sheets, and every animation was checked zoomed in, frame by frame, before it went in.

The game

The game is built in Godot 4.7: 74 scripts, written with AI help (Claude). An idle game is mostly numbers, and the numbers are where it breaks.

Coin totals outgrow anything a normal computer number can hold long before the bottom of the world. A phone that dies halfway through saving can wipe hours of digging. And a change that looks small in one place can make a boss impossible, or beatable by leaving the phone on a table.

So the game counts in its own big-number format, a mantissa and an exponent that never overflow. Every save carries a checksum, is written to a spare file first and keeps the last good copy, so a crash never leaves you with nothing. All 30 enemies, 10 bosses and 100 pieces of gear are data files, and a validator catches a missing field or a broken link between them before it reaches a build.

Balance is tuned by simulation. A bot plays the game from start to finish with every class on every difficulty, several runs each, and every boss is fought again with weaker gear and hands-off. A change goes in only when no class gets through without dying and no boss falls to a hero left idle. 47 automated test files run before every change, and the wiki is built from the same data, so its numbers are the game's.

Who decides

The design, the balance targets, which art made the cut and which got thrown away, and the play-testing on a real phone are the developer's. The AI tools made the work possible for one person; they did not make the decisions.
